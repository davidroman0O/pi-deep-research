# Determine a credible, normalized $/kW capital cost range for small modular reactors to support an investment or policy decision comparing SMRs against alternative generation technologies.

**Research date:** 2026-09-13 · **Sources analyzed:** 12 · **Evidence records:** 41 · **Verified claims:** 41

---

## Executive Summary

**Executive Summary**

Credible normalized SMR capital costs span $2,250–$20,139/kW; the range is a function of FOAK vs NOAK assumptions and whether financing is included, not of technology fundamentals. The strongest evidence is the cancelled UAMPS/NuScale project: cost escalated 74–75% to $9.2B and ~$20,139/kWe, versus vendor NOAK targets near $2,250–3,720/kW. Independent FOAK estimates ($11,600–20,139/kW) generally exceed realized large-nuclear costs (AP1000 at Vogtle, ~$13,000/kW). The biggest uncertainty is that no NOAK SMR exists; learning-curve discounts remain unvalidated. For policy or investment decisions, budget FOAK-level costs (~$12,000–20,000/kW), treat vendor targets as speculative, and require demonstrated serial-production evidence before crediting NOAK economics.

The credible overnight capital cost range spans roughly $2,250–$20,139/kW, anchored at the low end by GE-Hitachi's NOAK marketing target and at the high end by the cancelled NuScale/UAMPS project [12]. Vendor targets cluster at $2,850–$11,600/kW (inference — no direct source)[37], while independent and project-level estimates run $14,000–$28,986/kW, with MIT's 2024 FOAK benchmark at ~$14,000/kW excluding owner's cost [17][35]. Discrepancies reflect base year, currency, financing inclusion, and FOAK/NOAK status; confidence in individual figures is high, but the defensible planning range is wide, and decision-makers should anchor on project-level rather than marketing estimates.

## Why estimates diverge: FOAK vs NOAK escalation, financing inclusion, and normalization methodology

The roughly 9x spread among SMR capital estimates reflects three systematic differences.

| Program | Capital figure | Scope and conditions |
|---|---|---|
| NuScale/UAMPS | $6,833/kW (2015) → $20,130/kW (2023) [10] | FOAK, cancelled; project total rose 75% from $5.3B to $9.3B since 2021 [2]; modeling interest rate up ~200 bps since July 2020 [2] |
| GE-Hitachi BWRX-300 | $2,250/kW NOAK [12] | US dollars, nth-of-a-kind marketing target |
| X-energy Xe-100 | No explicit $/kW published [8]; costs rose 72% during 2021–2024 [10] | AACE Class 5 (-50%/+100%), 2020 USD, excludes capitalized financial costs [7] |

FOAK-to-NOAK discounting is the largest driver: the UAMPS trajectory (inference — no direct source) versus BWRX-300's target [12] implies a 3–9x FOAK premium, yet no NOAK SMR has been built to validate the assumed learning curve. Confidence is asymmetric: UAMPS is the only project-actual datapoint, and X-energy's estimate carries wide bounds [7]. Open evidence gaps — financing-structure effects, regulatory/licensing, thermal-integration, and supply-chain cost drivers — have no claim coverage here and remain decision risks.
