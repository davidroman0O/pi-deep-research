# Determine the realistic current overnight and total capital cost per installed kW for SMR designs that an investor, utility, or policymaker could reasonably use for procurement, rate-setting, or portfolio planning decisions in the near term

**Research date:** 2026-09-13 · **Sources analyzed:** 12 · **Evidence records:** 38 · **Verified claims:** 38

---

## Executive Summary

No single SMR capital-cost figure is procurement-ready. Credible planning numbers split by provenance: vendor/NOAK claims cluster at $2,850–3,700/kW, while independent FOAK estimates run $14,000–20,000/kW. The strongest evidence is NuScale's documented one-year escalation (UAMPS plant $5.3B→$9.3B, implying $20,139/kW) and MIT's bottom-up $14,000/kW BWRX-300 estimate. The biggest uncertainty is scope and dollar-year inconsistency: figures mix overnight vs. construction cost, include or exclude owner's cost, and omit IDC/financing, so true total capital cost exceeds overnight cost by an unstated margin. Recommendation: investors and policymakers should plan on independent FOAK figures with substantial contingency, treat vendor NOAK claims as unvalidated targets, and require scope- and dollar-year-reconciled estimates before rate-setting or procurement.

## 

Reported SMR capital costs span a roughly sevenfold range, split between vendor/NOAK claims and independent first-of-a-kind (FOAK) estimates (Table 1). Vendor and NOAK claims cluster near $2,900–3,700/kW [1], while independent FOAK estimates run $14,000–20,000/kW [2]. Aggregate techno-economic modeling places SMR capital cost at $3,985–4,844/kW at an $80–90/MWh LCOE, with FOAK at the high end [3]. Only two headline figures meet two-source corroboration: the NuScale FOAK value (INL and IEEFA) and BWRX-300's ~$2,900/kWe [4]. Dollar-year and gross/net capacity basis are unstated for most figures, including every BWRX-300, IMSR, Rolls-Royce, and X-energy value [5]. No single figure is therefore procurement-ready for rate-setting or portfolio planning without full scope (NSSS, balance of plant, financing, escalation) and dollar-year disclosure [6]. Confidence in the overall spread is high; confidence in any point estimate is low.

## Contradictions Detected

- **INL's October 2023 literature review (INL/RPT-23-72972) reports a NuScale/UAMPS FOAK overnight capital cost of $20,139/kWe in 2022 USD for a 6-module plant with 462 MWe capacity (reference [8]).** vs **NuScale's overnight capital cost is estimated at $3,720 per kW (source does not state dollar-year, capacity basis, or publication date).** — Both claims assert the same quantity — NuScale's overnight capital cost — but report mutually exclusive values: $20,139/kWe (2022 USD, FOAK, 6-module, 462 MWe, including financing) versus $3,720/kW. A factor-of-~5.4 gap cannot both be true for the same cost metric, so the content is logically opposed. Caveat: claim B states no dollar-year, capacity basis, or date, so the contradiction holds only insofar as B is read as a general claim about the same overnight capital cost quantity; if B's underlying basis (e.g., non-FOAK, different year, or excluding financing) were specified, it could instead describe a different world. As stated, however, the two values are incompatible for the same subject.
- **MIT's independent bottom-up estimate (MIT-ANP-TR-201, July 2024) puts GE-Hitachi BWRX-300 FOAK overnight cost at $14,000/kW (2024 USD), based on a best-estimate 80-month FOAK construction schedule and excluding owner's cost.** vs **GE-Hitachi BWRX-300 overnight capital cost is $2,980/kWe (dollar-year not stated in source; page updated May 5, 2026).** — Both claims assert the overnight capital cost of the same subject (GE-Hitachi BWRX-300) in the same metric ($/kW vs $/kWe are equivalent units for a power plant), but with mutually exclusive values: $14,000/kW (2024 USD) vs $2,980/kWe. The ~4.7x gap is far beyond what any dollar-year adjustment (2024 vs an unstated year) or net/gross basis difference could explain, so both cannot be true. Claim B's conditions are unspecified (dollar-year not stated, net/gross not specified, aggregator source) rather than affirmatively different, so they do not establish a different world; the claims are logically opposed on the same quantity.
