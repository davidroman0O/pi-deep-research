# Establish the current (2025–2026) capital cost per kW of small modular reactors — including credible ranges, cost structure, and drivers — to support investment, procurement, or technology-comparison decisions against large nuclear and other generation options.

**Research date:** 2026-09-14 · **Sources analyzed:** 7 · **Evidence records:** 42 · **Verified claims:** 39

## Executive Summary

Independent estimates place SMR capital costs far above vendor claims. Credible FOAK figures cluster at $11,000–$14,000/kW (MIT's bottom-up BWRX-300 estimate; derived ABWR benchmark), while NuScale's UAMPS project implies ~$20,000/kW all-in versus a $2,850/kW vendor overnight claim. Even at NOAK, SMRs remain ~45% costlier per kW than AP1000s, with model projections ranging from 105% to 294% of large-PWR costs. The strongest evidence is MIT's bottom-up study and UAMPS Class 3 estimates; the biggest uncertainty is FOAK cost-overrun risk, and no credible cost data exist for most designs. Recommendation: base procurement and investment decisions on independent bottom-up estimates, assume FOAK premiums persist, and treat vendor headline figures as unvalidated.

## Data quality, uncertainty, and evidence gaps

Confidence in available $/kW figures is low, as independent estimates materially exceed vendor claims. MIT/CANES places the BWRX-300 FOAK overnight cost at $14,000/kW (2024 USD, excluding owner's cost), while NuScale's $2,850/kW overnight claim is contradicted by IEEFA's assessment that the six-module project will likely exceed $3,000/kW; the UAMPS Class 3 estimate implies roughly $20,139/kW all-in. IEEFA argues actual construction cost will likely far exceed the Class 4 estimate, citing the untested FOAK design, module downsizing from 12 to six, pandemic supply-chain challenges, and escalation and financing costs [4].

Procurement termination triggers are explicit. The Class 3 Economic Competitiveness Test produced an LCOE above the $58/MWh (2020$) target, triggering UAMPS's option to withdraw [6]. The amended agreement sets a new $89/MWh (2022$) target, with 100% reimbursement on termination at Class 2 PCE or COLA submittal (inference — no direct source). UAMPS must reach 80% subscription (370 MW of 462 MW) by end-2023 [6].

Evidence gaps persist: EIA's SMR case is representative, not OEM-attributable, and no verified data cover IEA, NEA, Darlington, or Wylfa, or cross-regional cost differentials.
