# Determine the current (2025–2026) capital cost per kW of small modular reactors across leading designs and regions, so that an energy investor or utility planner can benchmark SMR capital costs against large nuclear and other generation options for procurement or investment decisions.

**Research date:** 2026-09-14 · **Sources analyzed:** 12 · **Evidence records:** 72 · **Verified claims:** 69

---

## Executive Summary

**Executive summary**

Decision-relevant bottom line: SMR capital costs are not yet competitive. The credible planning range for first-of-a-kind (FOAK) projects is roughly $14,000–$20,000/kW, anchored by MIT's independent BWRX-300 estimate ($14,000–$16,700/kW) and the cancelled UAMPS NuScale project (~$20,139/kW). Vendor and nth-of-a-kind (NOAK) figures ($2,900–$5,100/kW) run 2–7x lower and should not anchor procurement decisions. The strongest evidence is the UAMPS case: costs rose 75% and the project was terminated. The biggest uncertainty is the FOAK-to-NOAK learning curve—assumptions range from roughly 50% cost reduction to only ~14%—and no component-level or regional cost data exist. Recommendation: plan on FOAK costs well above the AP1000 brownfield benchmark (~$7,861/kW) and treat NOAK savings as unproven.

## Project reality check: the UAMPS CFPP case, EPC estimates vs outcomes, and what is included in each $/kW figure

The only fully documented SMR project—NuScale's UAMPS Carbon Free Power Project (CFPP)—was mutually terminated on November 8, 2023, after member utilities withdrew and subscription fell short of the 80% threshold [8]. Its IEEFA-derived estimate of $20,139/kW (2022 USD) for six 77-MW modules (462 MW total) rests on a $9.3 billion construction cost that rose 75% from $5.3 billion between 2021 and January 2023 [1][4][7]. Critically, this figure includes financing costs, so it is not a pure overnight capital cost (OCC) [4]. The target power price escalated from $55/MWh to $89/MWh (a 53% rise), and the operational date slipped from 2026 to 2029 [6][7]. DOE had committed $1.4 billion in cost-share, with $232 million disbursed by cancellation [6]. By contrast, X-energy's ARDP-normalized ~$7,800 $2020/kWe is a FOAK demonstration figure bundling design, licensing, testing, and fuel-fabrication costs [1]. ORNL conventions define OCC as base construction cost plus contingency, excluding interest during construction and escalation [12].

Claim A states the pre-update price was $55/MWh (updated to $89/MWh), while Claim B states the previous estimate was $58/MWh. Same subject, same scope/date, and compatible conditions, but numerically opposed values ($55 vs $58), so they cannot both be true.
