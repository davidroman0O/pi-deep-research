# Determine the realistic current capital cost per kW (overnight and all-in, including financing) of small modular reactors across leading designs, distinguishing first-of-a-kind from nth-of-a-kind estimates, to support an investment or procurement decision comparing SMRs against alternative generation technologies.

**Research date:** 2026-09-14 · **Sources analyzed:** 13 · **Evidence records:** 84 · **Verified claims:** 83

---

## Executive Summary

Realistic current first-of-a-kind SMR capital costs are roughly $7,000–$14,000/kW overnight, with all-in costs 20–40% higher; UAMPS reached $20,139/kW all-in before cancellation. Independent estimates (EIA/NREL $7,300–$8,900/kW; MIT BWRX-300 $14,000/kW) run 2.5–5x above vendor claims, and representative estimates rose ~23% in real terms since 2021. The biggest uncertainty is NOAK: targets rest on unverified learning-curve and factory assumptions, with deployment volume and timing unknown; MIT finds NOAK SMRs remain ~45% costlier than NOAK AP1000. Recommendation: treat vendor NOAK prices as contingent targets, not current costs; benchmark against large LWR (~$7,800/kW) and gas/renewables ($1,000–$1,900/kW); verify design-specific figures from primary sources before committing.

## Cost escalation since 2021 and the empirical record: UAMPS cancellation and Darlington

The UAMPS/NuScale project is the clearest FOAK escalation record. All-in cost (including financing) rose from ~$5,833/kW at the 2014 12-module concept (720 MW, $4.2B) to ~$8,472/kW by NRC design approval in September 2020 [8], then to ~$20,130/kW at the January 2023 Class 3 estimate for the downsized 462 MW plant ($9.3B) [7][8]; net of $4.2B in DOE/IRA benefits it was ~$11,039/kW [7]. The Class 3 Economic Competitiveness Test produced an LCOE above the $58/MWh (2020$) target, triggering withdrawal rights; an amended agreement reset the target to $89/MWh (2022$) [7]. On 7 November 2023, 26 of 50 members voted to terminate after subscription reached only 26% versus the 80% (370 MW) threshold [8][7].

Darlington's 4×300 MWe BWRX-300 program totals CAD20.9B (~CAD17,400/kW), including interest and escalation over the 10-year horizon, with contingency unpublished [9]. The first unit plus CAD1.6B shared infrastructure is CAD7.7B; the fourth unit is forecast ~33% cheaper [9]. First unit in-service is 2030 [9].

## Contradictions Detected

- **The light-water SMR (LW-SMR) plant (12x77 MWe modules, 924 MWe total) has an overnight capital cost of $4,844/kW.** vs **Independent overnight capital cost estimates for the representative SMR plant span $7,288 to $8,942/kW (2023 dollars), i.e., roughly $7,300-$8,900/kW.** — Both claims assert independent overnight capital cost (OCC) estimates for SMR plants in the same units and currency basis (2023 $/kW). Claim A gives a specific value of $4,844/kW for a light-water SMR plant, while Claim B gives a range of $7,288–$8,942/kW for the representative SMR plant. The $4,844/kW figure falls well outside Claim B's stated range, so the two propositions are logically incompatible: they cannot both be true for the same class of plant. Although Claim A carries specific methodological conditions (bottom-up estimate, 10% owner's costs and contingency, excludes IDC, generic LW-SMR), both claims concern the same subject matter (independent OCC estimates for SMRs in 2023 dollars) and the values are mutually exclusive, making this a contradiction rather than a duplicate, support, or unrelated pair.

## Scenario Model: NOAK all-in capital cost per kW (2025$, including financing) for leading SMR designs (BWRX-300-class), the pivotal FOAK→NOAK learning-rate trajectory

**Base estimate:** ~$11,000/kW (2025$) all-in NOAK central estimate — triangulated from Darlington 4th-unit all-in CAD13,667/kW (≈USD9,800/kW, 2025) and EIA AEO2023 SMR overnight $8,349/kW (2022$) escalated to 2025$ with ~1.2 financing multiplier; FOAK all-in ≈$14,700/kW (Darlington unit 1, 2025) and ≈$20,130/kW (UAMPS/NuScale, 2023); overnight NOAK band $7,300–8,900/kW (2023$, EIA/NREL) vs INL NOAK band $4,000–7,000/kWe (2019$). Comparator alternatives (2025$, EIA AEO2026): CCGT $1,032–1,086/kW, solar+storage $1,903/kW, onshore wind $1,712/kW.
