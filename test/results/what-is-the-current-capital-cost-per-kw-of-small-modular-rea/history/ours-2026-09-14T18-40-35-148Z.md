# Determine realistic, decision-grade current capital cost per kW ranges for small modular reactors (SMRs) to support an investment or procurement decision comparing SMRs against large-scale nuclear and other generation alternatives.

**Research date:** 2026-09-14 · **Sources analyzed:** 16 · **Evidence records:** 93 · **Verified claims:** 87

---

## Executive Summary

Decision-grade answer: no single credible $/kW figure exists. Realistic mature-SMR overnight costs cluster at $4,700–$8,350/kW (EPIC, EIA), versus vendor NOAK targets of $2,250–$3,900/kW and realized FOAK projects of $17,000–$20,000/kW including financing (UAMPS, Darlington). Strongest evidence is EIA's same-basis comparison: SMRs at $8,349/kW are ~7% above large LWRs at $7,777/kW (2022$), so SMRs are not capital-cost-competitive with large nuclear. Biggest uncertainty is the unquantified FOAK-to-NOAK learning curve and WACC/financing sensitivity, which drove UAMPS's 75% cost escalation. Recommendation: base the decision on LCOE and financing/policy structure (tax credits, cost-sharing), not $/kW, and treat vendor NOAK figures as unvalidated targets.

## Scenario Model: SMR all-in capital cost per kW (2025 USD, FOAK, incl. financing & owner's costs)

**Base estimate:** $13,000/kW (2025 USD) — central FOAK all-in estimate; anchored on OPG Darlington 4-unit $20.9B CAD/1,200 MW ≈ $13,100/kW (2025$) and EIA AEO2023 overnight $8,349/kW (2022$) + ~30% financing/owner uplift (MIT NCET owner's cost 40–45% of EPC; EIA Case 9 owner's services 20% of EPC); UAMPS $20,139/kW (2022$) ≈ $22,000/kW (2025$) caps the observed range
