# Determine the current (2025–2026) capital cost per kW of small modular reactors, including credible ranges, cost drivers, and how they compare with large nuclear and alternative generation technologies, to support investment, procurement, and energy-planning decisions.

**Research date:** 2026-09-14 · **Sources analyzed:** 11 · **Evidence records:** 76 · **Verified claims:** 76

---

## Executive Summary

SMR overnight capital costs span roughly 2,000–8,000 $/kW for NOAK-to-mid estimates, with realized FOAK figures far higher (UAMPS at ~20,000 $/kW including financing). On a like-for-like basis, SMRs are only ~7% more expensive than large LWRs per kW but roughly 4–6x CCGT and ~4x solar/wind. The strongest evidence is EIA's 8,349 $/kW reference; the biggest uncertainty is the FOAK-to-NOAK learning gap, which varies ~4x between generic projections and static estimates. Recommendation: base procurement on EIA-class figures, treat vendor targets (e.g., 2,850 $/kW) as optimistic, and price in substantial FOAK and financing premiums.

## SMR overnight capital cost per kW: design-level estimates and FOAK/NOAK ranges

NuScale is the only design with multiple published overnight capital cost (OCC) estimates, spanning an order of magnitude (Table 1). Vendor and independent first-of-a-kind (FOAK) estimates differ by roughly 5–7×, so vendor per-kW claims are not decision-grade.

**Table 1. Published SMR overnight capital cost estimates**

| Design / basis | OCC | Plant basis | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| NuScale VOYGR (vendor, 2020) | 2,850 $2018/kWe | 12-unit, 924 MWe | Excludes financing [1] |
| NuScale iPWR (Stewart & Shirvan, 2021) | 3,856 $2018/kWe | 12-unit, 685 MWe | Excludes financing [1] |
| NuScale iPWR (2015-era) | 5,100 $2015/kWe | 12-unit, 570 MWe | [1] |
| NuScale UAMPS FOAK (IEEFA, 2023) | 20,139 $2022/kWe | 6-unit, 462 MWe | Includes financing [1] |
| Generic SMR FOAK / NOAK | 3,800 / 2,000 $2020/kWe | 4-unit, 600 MWe | [1] |
| EIA AEO2023 SMR | 8,349 $/kW (2022$) | 600 MW | Includes 1.10 optimism factor [11] |

Generic FOAK/NOAK estimates sit well below EIA's figure [1][11]. No OCC is published for BWRX-300, Xe-100, IMSR, or Holtec SMR-300 [1][2]; Rolls-Royce SMR is quoted only as £1.8–2.5 billion total plant cost for 470 MWe [2], with NSI scoring its overnight cost qualitatively, citing larger balance-of-plant and nuclear-grade containment as drivers [3]. Confidence is moderate: all sources predate the 2025–2026 window, and the $3,720/kW figure carries no FOAK/NOAK designation or named origin [2].

## Like-for-like capital cost comparison: SMRs vs large LWRs and alternative generation

On a consistent overnight basis (EIA AEO2023, 2022$), a 600 MW SMR costs $8,349/kW, roughly 7% above the $7,777/kW for a 2,156 MW large LWR [11]. Both figures include contingency and optimism factors but exclude interest during construction (IDC), learning effects, and regional multipliers [11]. The only AP1000-specific estimate, $7,861/kW net (2023$), is a total capital cost including IDC and is therefore not directly comparable [9].

| Technology | Capacity | $/kW (2022$, overnight) |
|---|---|---|
| SMR | 600 MW | 8,349 [11] |
| Large LWR | 2,156 MW | 7,777 [11] |
| Offshore wind | 400 MW | 6,672 [11] |
| Onshore wind | 200 MW | 2,098 [11] |
| Solar PV (tracking) | 150 MW | 1,448 [11] |
| Gas CCGT | 418 MW | 1,330 [11] |
| Battery storage | 50 MW | 1,270 [11] |

Non-nuclear generation is roughly four to six times cheaper per kW than SMRs; battery storage is expressed per kW of power capacity, not per kWh of energy [11]. Confidence is moderate: the like-for-like set rests on a single EIA source in 2022$, earlier EIA vintages (2016$) report different absolute values, and the AEO2025 SMR reference plant is 480 MW net, not 600 MW [10][9].

## Learning-curve projections, estimate credibility, and regional variation

Learning-curve projections span a wide range. Generic SMR estimates imply a FOAK-to-NOAK overnight-cost multiplier of roughly 1.9x, from 3,800 to 2,000 $2020/kWe for a four-unit, 600 MWe plant [1]. Vendor NOAK-style targets sit far lower, while EIA's static estimate excludes learning effects entirely [11]:

| Estimate | $/kW | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Generic SMR FOAK (4×600 MWe) | 3,800 | $2020, excl. financing [1] |
| Generic SMR NOAK | 2,000 | $2020, excl. financing [1] |
| NuScale FOAK (UAMPS, 6-unit) | 20,139 | $2022, incl. financing [1] |
| NuScale vendor (12-unit) | 2,850 | $2018, excl. financing [1] |
| EIA SMR (600 MW) | 8,349 | $2022, excl. IDC/learning [11] |

The FOAK-to-vendor gap (~7x) mixes learning, financing, and estimate-basis differences. Credibility caveats apply: EIA applies a 1.10 technological optimism factor to unproven designs and excludes regional multipliers [11]; AP1000 figures rely on EPC escalation methodology [9], with adjustment factors varying across U.S. sites [9]. Regionally, the UK Rolls-Royce SMR programme remains pre-FID; DESNZ flags first-of-a-kind risk and cost uncertainty [5], with no numeric $/kW published [3].

## Contradictions Detected

- **NuScale VOYGR overnight capital cost of 2,850 $2018/kWe for a 12-unit, 924 MWe plant, per NuScale announcement reported by World Nuclear News (November 2020), as tabulated in the INL literature review.** vs **NuScale's overnight capital cost is estimated at $3,720 per kW.** — Both claims assert the overnight capital cost of the same subject (NuScale VOYGR) and give incompatible values: $2,850/kWe (2018 dollars, 12-unit/924 MWe, Nov 2020) versus $3,720/kW. The ~30% gap cannot be reconciled as the same proposition, and Claim B provides no date, dollar-year, FOAK/NOAK designation, or source that would place it in a different scenario/world. Since the values are mutually exclusive for the same metric, the claims contradict.
