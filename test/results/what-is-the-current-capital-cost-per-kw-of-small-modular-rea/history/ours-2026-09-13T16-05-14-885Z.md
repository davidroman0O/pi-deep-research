# Determine the current capital cost per kW (overnight and total, including financing) of small modular reactors as of 2026, with credible ranges and drivers, to support investment, procurement, or policy decisions comparing SMRs against alternative generation technologies.

**Research date:** 2026-09-13 · **Sources analyzed:** 15 · **Evidence records:** 50 · **Verified claims:** 48

---

## Executive Summary

Decision-relevant bottom line: credible FOAK SMR capital costs cluster at $14,000–20,000/kW (2024–2026 USD equivalent), anchored by TVA's $17,949/kW OCC, NuScale's $20,139/kWe, and OPG's ~CAD17,400/kW total cost. Vendor NOAK targets of $2,250–3,000/kW imply a 5–8x learning-curve discount that no verified deployment supports. The dominant uncertainty is whether serial production can close that gap, compounded by methodological heterogeneity (no figures natively in 2026 USD; financing inclusion varies) and escalation risk (NuScale +74% in two years). Recommendation: for investment, procurement, or policy decisions, budget at $14,000–20,000/kW for near-term FOAK commitments and treat sub-$6,000/kW as contingent on demonstrated multi-unit fleet deployment.

Financing inclusion dominates the spread: NuScale's $2,850/kWe excluding financing versus $20,139/kWe including it [1]. The evidence base lacks line-item capital structure and 2026 USD normalization—a material gap for procurement analysis. Confidence is moderate-to-high for NuScale and BWRX-300; low for Xe-100 and IMSR.

## FOAK vs NOAK Economics, Financing Effects, and Cost Drivers

FOAK penalties dominate SMR capital-cost uncertainty. NuScale's 6-unit UAMPS FOAK OCC reached $20,139/kWe (2022 USD, including financing) [1], roughly comparable to the two-reactor Vogtle project on a $/kW basis [14]. GE-Hitachi's BWRX-300 shows wide dispersion across estimates (Table 1), with OPG's committed four-unit Darlington fleet averaging ~CAD17,400/kW including interest and contingency [10][11] — roughly 6–8x the vendor's original $2,250/kW serial-production target [10].

**Table 1. BWRX-300 and NuScale capital-cost estimates**

| Estimate | $/kW | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| NuScale VOYGR FOAK (UAMPS) | $20,139/kWe | 2022 USD, incl. financing [1] |
| BWRX-300 FOAK (MIT CANES) | $14,000/kW | 2024 USD, excl. owner's cost [4] |
| BWRX-300 FOAK (TVA) | $17,949/kW | 2024 USD, base construction only [5] |
| BWRX-300 vendor target | $2,250/kW | Serial production [10] |

Financing and escalation drive much of the gap. NuScale's CFPP cost rose 74% from $5.3B (2021) to $9.2B (January 2023) [8], lifting the target price from $58/MWh to $89/MWh (2022 USD) [14], or $102/MWh at 2% inflation [14]. X-energy costs rose 72% over 2021–2024, though no absolute $/kW was disclosed [5]. Learning effects appear modest: OPG's fourth BWRX-300 unit (~CAD13,700/kW) is ~33% cheaper than the first [11]. Confidence is high on reported figures but low on NOAK trajectories, which rest on unquantified vendor assumptions.

## Contradictions Detected

- **NuScale's overnight capital cost is estimated at $3,720 per kW (Gitnux SMR statistics page; no publication date or currency year specified, report-level attribution across 21 datasets).** vs **The NuScale SMR is about as expensive on a dollars-per-kilowatt basis ($20,139/kW) as the two-reactor Vogtle project in Georgia.** — Both claims assert the same metric — NuScale's overnight capital cost in USD per kW — but give mutually exclusive values: $3,720/kW (Claim A) versus $20,139/kW (Claim B, UAMPS Class 3 estimate). They cannot both be true for the same quantity. Although the sources and dates differ (Gitnux aggregation with no date vs. IEEFA January 2023 analysis), neither claim introduces a different geography, unit, or scenario that would place them in different worlds; they are competing estimates of the same overnight cost figure, so the logically opposed values constitute a contradiction.
- **MIT CANES (MIT-ANP-TR-201, July 2024) estimates the GE-Hitachi BWRX-300 FOAK overnight capital cost at $14,000/kW in 2024 USD, without owner's cost, based on a best-estimate 80-month FOAK construction schedule.** vs **TVA's recent filings (cited in David Schlissel's 2025 answer testimony before the Colorado Public Utilities Commission, reported by Clean Energy Group) estimate the GE-Hitachi BWRX-300 overnight cost at $17,949 per kilowatt in 2024 dollars, covering base construction costs only and excluding financing costs and inflation during construction.** — Both claims assert the same atomic quantity — the GE-Hitachi BWRX-300 overnight capital cost in 2024 USD, excluding financing-type costs (Claim A: FOAK overnight cost without owner's cost; Claim B: base construction cost excluding financing and escalation). The subject, currency year, and cost-basis scope are compatible, but the values are materially different ($14,000/kW vs $17,949/kW), so they cannot both be accurate. The differing sources (MIT CANES vs TVA filing) are competing estimates of the same quantity rather than different-scope claims, so this is a true contradiction rather than unrelated or qualifying claims.
