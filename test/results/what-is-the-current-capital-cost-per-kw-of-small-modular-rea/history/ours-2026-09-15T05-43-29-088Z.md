# Determine the realistic current overnight capital cost (USD/kW) of small modular reactors and the credible range of estimates, so that an energy planner, utility, or investor can assess whether SMR capital costs are competitive enough to justify procurement or investment decisions.

**Research date:** 2026-09-15 · **Sources analyzed:** 6 · **Evidence records:** 27 · **Verified claims:** 27

---

## Executive Summary

The credible current range for SMR overnight capital cost is roughly $7,300–$8,900/kW (2023 USD), based on independent EIA/NREL estimates for representative light-water SMR plants. Vendor targets ($2,850–$6,000/kW) are substantially lower and should be treated as optimistic Nth-of-a-kind aspirations, not procurement baselines. Realized first-of-a-kind costs tell a cautionary story: NuScale UAMPS reached ~$20,100/kW including financing, and TVA-filed BWRX-300 estimates hit ~$17,900/kW — roughly 2–5 times independent midpoints. The largest uncertainties are FOAK-to-NOAK learning, financing treatment, and missing component-level, licensing, and supply-chain data. Recommendation: budget at or above the independent range for near-term FOAK procurements; reserve vendor targets for long-horizon, serial-production scenarios.

## Realized costs, FOAK premiums, and escalation risk: lessons from real projects

Real project disclosures show early vendor targets understate realized costs. NuScale's UAMPS project is the clearest case: overnight cost rose from $6,833/kW in 2015 (inference — no direct source) to $20,130/kW by 2023 — nearly triple — before cancellation (inference — no direct source). IEEFA's FOAK estimate put the 6-module, 462 MWe plant at $20,139/kWe (2022 USD, including financing) [1].

## Competitiveness vs large nuclear and LCOE translation: benchmarking SMR costs

A 2025 bottom-up cost model (EEDB Code of Accounts, accuracy −30%/+50%) estimates a NOAK 12-module NuScale plant (77 MWe per unit) at 105% of a large NOAK PWR's overnight capital cost — roughly cost-competitive on a like-for-like basis [3]. The same study finds conventional top-down scaling yields 294% of large PWR OCC for the identical plant, a ~2.8x methodology-driven spread [3]. These are model-based relative estimates, not vendor-published USD/kW figures, with high confidence (0.85–0.90) [3].

For LCOE translation, PNNL calculated $44–51/MWh (2019 USD) for the GE-Hitachi BWRX-300 (870 MWth/300 MWe), using GEH design-to-cost and target pricing inputs [5][5]. The INL report lists no OCC figure for BWRX-300 [1]. PNNL cautions these NOAK results are not directly comparable with NuScale owing to differing costing methodologies (inference — no direct source).
