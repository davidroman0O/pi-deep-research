# Determine the current (2026) capital cost per kW of small modular reactors — including credible reported figures, cost drivers, and comparability caveats — to support an investment, procurement, or policy decision on SMR deployment.

**Research date:** 2026-09-14 · **Sources analyzed:** 8 · **Evidence records:** 42 · **Verified claims:** 41

---

## Executive Summary

Current SMR capital costs are not reportable as a single 2026 $/kW figure. Verified estimates span roughly $2,250/kW (GE-Hitachi target) to $20,100/kW (NuScale/UAMPS first-of-a-kind, including financing), with INL's generic range at $4,000–7,000/kWe. The strongest evidence is UAMPS: costs rose 75% to $9.3B, triggering cancellation, while learning-curve analyses project 35–50% FOAK-to-NOAK reductions. The biggest uncertainty is comparability: figures mix currencies, dollar vintages, financing, and estimate maturity, and several designs have no verified OCC. For decision-grade use, normalize scope and escalation, load FOAK contingency, and benchmark against INL/IEA anchors (~$4,500/kW) rather than vendor targets.

## Scenario Model: Overnight capital cost (OCC) per kWe of first-commercial SMR deployments, 2026 USD, excluding financing costs

**Base estimate:** ~$12,000/kWe (2026 USD, overnight, excl. financing) — triangulated central FOAK estimate from: UAMPS/NuScale $20,139/kW 2022 incl. financing (~$15k/kW overnight); OPG Darlington C$20.9B/1,200MW 2024 incl. interest (~US$13.5k/kW) and C$7.7B first-unit (~US$18.7k/kW incl. shared infra); INL literature range $4,000–7,000/kWe 2019 (~$5,000–9,000/kWe 2026); NOAK anchors $2,850–3,856/kWe 2018

| Scenario | Assumption | 2026 | 2030 | 2035 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative (0% effective learning, FOAK contingency retained, schedule slip) | FOAK contingency up to 55% retained (source [8]); no serial-production learning before 2030; Darlington-style schedule slip (2028→2029, source [6]) delays NOAK beyond 2035; only 2nd–4th-unit 5% reductions (Wilton 2012) materialize by 2035 | $13,000/kWe (FOAK, full contingency) | $13,500/kWe (first deployments at FOAK cost + escalation) | $12,000/kWe (limited ~8% learning from early units) |
| Base (4.5% learning per doubling, EMWG methodology) | 4.5% technological learning rate per doubling (INL model, source [8]) → ~50% TCIC reduction FOAK→NOAK; first unit −15%, units 2–4 −5% each (Wilton 2012); max NOAK reduction ~35–40% (source [8]) | $12,000/kWe (central FOAK estimate) | $10,200/kWe (first commercial units ~15% below FOAK) | $7,500/kWe (NOAK ~37.5% below FOAK, midpoint of 35–40%) |
