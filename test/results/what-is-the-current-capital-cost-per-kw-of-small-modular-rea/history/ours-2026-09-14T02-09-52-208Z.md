# Determine the realistic current capital cost range (US$/kW) of small modular reactors, decomposed by design, deployment stage, and region, to inform an investment/procurement decision on whether and which SMR technology to pursue relative to alternative power generation options.

**Research date:** 2026-09-14 · **Sources analyzed:** 13 · **Evidence records:** 57 · **Verified claims:** 53

---

## Executive Summary

**Executive summary.** Realistic SMR capital costs converge on ~$12,600–17,400/kW all-in for first-of-a-kind units; even independent NOAK estimates (~$7,600–9,500/kW) are 2–3× higher than vendor targets (~$2,250–3,720/kW). The strongest evidence comes from actual FOAK projects—Vogtle (~$15,000/kW OCC) and Darlington (CAD20.9B all-in)—and from realized serial learning (fourth BWRX-300 unit ~33% cheaper). The biggest uncertainty is the unvalidated vendor NOAK trajectories, accounting-basis inconsistency, and Class 5 estimate ranges. **Recommendation:** budget investment decisions on all-in FOAK costs ($12,000–17,000/kW), treat vendor NOAK figures as planning upside only, and favor designs with committed fleet deployment (e.g., BWRX-300 Darlington); absent cost-sharing, large-scale nuclear or gas-backed renewables remain more competitive near term.

## Contradictions Detected

- **Per the Generation IV Cost Estimating Guidelines, the capitalized financial cost (including escalation, fees capitalized with the plant, and interest during construction) is not included in the Xe-100 Overnight Cost and LCOE.** vs **The all-inclusive plant capital cost used for LCOE equals base construction cost plus contingency, escalation, IDC, owner's cost (including utility start-up), commissioning (non-utility start-up), and initial fuel core costs (Generation IV EMWG 2006).** — Both claims address whether capitalized financial costs (escalation and IDC) are part of the capital cost basis used for LCOE under the Generation IV cost estimating framework. Claim A asserts these financial costs are NOT included in the Xe-100 Overnight Cost and LCOE, while Claim B asserts the all-inclusive plant capital cost used for LCOE DOES include escalation and IDC (along with contingency, owner's cost, commissioning, and initial fuel core). On the shared point of whether escalation/IDC enter the LCOE capital basis, the two claims are logically opposed, with compatible conditions (both cite Gen IV guidelines/EMWG 2006).
