# Determine the realistic current capital cost per kW (overnight and total, first-of-a-kind vs. Nth-of-a-kind) of leading small modular reactor designs, so the requester can benchmark SMR economics against large nuclear and other generation technologies for a deployment or investment decision.

**Research date:** 2026-09-15 · **Sources analyzed:** 11 · **Evidence records:** 67 · **Verified claims:** 65

---

## Executive Summary

Leading SMR capital costs span roughly $2,000–$20,000/kW, with the spread driven by FOAK vs. NOAK status, overnight vs. total scope, and year-dollar. The strongest evidence comes from committed projects: UAMPS reached ~$20,100/kW total (cancelled) and OPG Darlington implies ~$18,700/kW at FID — roughly 5.7x GE Hitachi's early ~$3,300/kW estimate — while NOAK overnight figures such as NuScale VOYGR at $2,850/kW (2018$) sit at the low end. The biggest uncertainty is comparability: mixed currencies, year-dollars, and scope definitions, with no gas or renewables benchmarks and unquantified licensing costs. Recommendation: benchmark on total capital cost including IDC and owner's costs, normalize to a common year-dollar, and treat vendor NOAK overnight figures as optimistic planning targets.

## Cost framework and normalization: making $/kW figures comparable

Across DOE/ORNL and EMWG frameworks, overnight capital cost includes EPC cost (with contingency) and owner's costs but excludes escalation and interest during construction (IDC), assuming commercial operation in the estimate year [7]. All-in (total) capital cost expands overnight cost by adding IDC and escalation, expressed in current-year dollars [4]. Contingency is calculated as a percentage of base construction cost, varying by component, design stage, and reliability [5]. Owner's costs include site-specific grid connection; whether such development costs are capitalized or expensed materially affects comparability (inference — no direct source). Decommissioning is excluded from both capital-cost lines, appearing separately in levelized generation cost [4]. For rate-regulated utilities, IDC is reported as AFUDC, accumulated as capital and recovered in rate base (inference — no direct source). EMWG defines total capital investment as overnight cost plus IDC, computed from overnight cost, project lead time, and a 5% yearly real WACC [7]. A worked NOAK 600 MWe SMR example: $2,867M overnight ($4,778/kW) versus $3,084M all-in, an 8.69% IDC factor [4]. These scope and year-dollar differences largely explain the ~7x spread (roughly $2,000–$20,000/kW) across vendor estimates; confidence in definitions is high, moderate for applied figures.

## Contradictions Detected

- **GE Hitachi BWRX-300 (870 MWth/300 MWe) LCOE of 44-51 $2019/MW-hr; the INL review reports no $/kW overnight capital cost figure for BWRX-300.** vs **BWRX-300 LCOE projections are in the $60 to $70 per MWh range.** — Both claims concern the same subject (BWRX-300 LCOE) and neither states conditions incompatible with the other (Claim A specifies 2019 USD and a source; Claim B states only that it is LCOE, not capital cost; no differing date/year-dollar is asserted). The asserted LCOE ranges are logically opposed and mutually exclusive: 44–51 $2019/MWh vs 60–70 $/MWh, with no overlap. Since the ranges cannot both hold for the same quantity under compatible conditions, they conflict rather than reinforce or duplicate.
