# Determine whether to invest in or license solid-state battery cell technology by assessing which recent energy-density breakthroughs are real, scalable, and commercially viable within the next 3–5 years.

**Research date:** 2026-08-13 · **Sources analyzed:** 12 · **Evidence records:** 43 · **Verified claims:** 38

---

## Executive Summary

As of 2026, independent testing (VTT) measured Donut Lab's claimed 400 Wh/kg cell at just 298 Wh/kg—an advanced Li-ion, not solid-state—highlighting the core due-diligence risk: manufacturer claims routinely exceed verified performance. Credible cell-level figures cluster at 272–425 Wh/kg and 823–1,299 Wh/L, with lithium-metal anodes enabling the highs, yet demonstrated cycle life remains ~200 cycles, far below commercial requirements. The biggest uncertainty is scalability: no pilot-line, cost, or certification data exists, and only one cost estimate (SOLiDIFY, ~€150/kWh) is available. Recommendation: do not invest or license based on headline density claims; require third-party cell-level validation, cycle-life data, and manufacturing maturity before committing.

## Reality Check: Which Reported Energy-Density Breakthroughs Are Credible vs. Disputed

Since 2024, no solid-state energy-density claim has been independently verified; the only third-party test located (VTT, Finland) disputed the claim it examined. Donut Lab's 400 Wh/kg claim, announced at CES 2026 [4], was measured by VTT at 297–298 Wh/kg [6][7], though VTT did not weigh the cells to determine mass or volume [4]. Company-reported figures remain unverified: SoftBank/Enpower Japan reported ~350 Wh/kg cell-level and 392 Wh/kg electrode-level all-solid-state lithium-metal cells [1], with volumetric density stated only as on par with liquid Li-ion cells (inference — no direct source). TDK announced 1,000 Wh/L material for wearables [2], and the SOLiDIFY consortium reported 1,070 Wh/L [3], about 20% above the ~800 Wh/L of standard Li-ion [3]. Modeled values include Sepion's 365 Wh/kg/885 Wh/L (40 µm Li foil) [8] and 425 Wh/kg/1,299 Wh/L (copper-foil anode) (inference — no direct source), while an LLZO modeling study projects only 272 Wh/kg gravimetric [11] and 823 Wh/L volumetric [11].

## Technology Architecture: Anode and Electrolyte Chemistry and Cell- vs. Material-Level Density

Independent testing of Donut Lab's disputed cell found it contains lithium, with voltage curves resembling conventional lithium-ion batteries, suggesting an enhanced lithium-ion design rather than a true solid-state cell [6]; VTT identified an NCM cathode and graphite anode [7]. This matters because lithium metal underpins the highest claimed densities. SoftBank/Enpower reported ~350 Wh/kg cell-level and 392 Wh/kg electrode-level all-solid-state lithium-metal cells, with volumetric density stated only qualitatively as on par with liquid Li-ion [1]. TDK's oxide-based cell with lithium alloy anode reached 1,000 Wh/L [2]. SOLiDIFY's lithium-metal pouch cell achieved 1,070 Wh/L, about 20% higher than ~800 Wh/L standard Li-ion [3]. Sepion's modeled cells range from 365–425 Wh/kg [8]. Yet a 2025 modeling study found LLZO-based cells reach only 272 Wh/kg, marginally above the 250–270 Wh/kg of current Li-ion [11]. Confidence is limited: most figures are company-reported or modeled, with no third-party verification.

## Scalability, Cycle Life, and Cost: The Gap to Commercial Production

Cycle-life evidence remains sparse and early-stage, with no operating-temperature-range data provided. SoftBank demonstrated 200 cycles at 392 Wh/kg at the electrode level [1], targeting 400 Wh/kg by FY2024 and then over 1,000 cycles [1], while conceding large pouch cells still tend to short circuit during cycling [1]. Donut Lab claimed 100,000 cycles and 5-minute charging for a sodium-based, critical-material-free design [7], but VTT tested only seven cycles, insufficient to substantiate the claim [4][10], which remains unverified [6].

| Cycle-life evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| SoftBank (electrode level) | 200 cycles demonstrated at 392 Wh/kg [1] |
| Donut Lab claim | 100,000 cycles, 5-minute charge [7] |
| VTT test (damaged DL2 cell) | 54.66% capacity loss (24.689→11.194 Ah) after 50 cycles at 5C; efficiency 89.6%→83.0%; 17% thickness swelling [10] |

On cost, the SOLiDIFY consortium estimated €150/kWh [3]. Confidence is high (0.85–0.92), but most figures are company-reported; only the VTT test is third-party.

## Contradictions Detected

- **Donut Lab claimed its solid-state battery achieves 400 Wh/kg energy density.** vs **Third-party testing confirmed the Donut Lab battery achieved an energy density of 297 Wh/kg, falling short of the claimed 400 Wh/kg.** — Both claims concern the same subject (Donut Lab's solid-state battery energy density) under compatible conditions (the same battery, same metric). Claim A asserts 400 Wh/kg, while Claim B asserts independent testing measured 297 Wh/kg, which is logically opposed to the 400 Wh/kg figure. Same subject, compatible conditions, and opposed values constitute a true contradiction.
- **Donut Lab claimed its solid-state battery achieves 400 Wh/kg energy density.** vs **The Donut Lab battery was found to be an advanced lithium-ion battery (NCM cathode, graphite anode), not the claimed sodium-ion solid-state battery.** — Both claims concern the same subject (the Donut Lab battery). Claim A asserts the battery is a solid-state battery (as claimed by the company, achieving 400 Wh/kg). Claim B asserts the battery is actually an advanced lithium-ion battery (NCM cathode, graphite anode) and NOT the claimed sodium-ion solid-state battery. The battery-type characterization in Claim B is logically opposed to the solid-state characterization in Claim A under compatible conditions (same battery, same testing context), so Claim B contradicts Claim A's solid-state proposition.
