# Assess how carbon pricing affects the cost competitiveness and market position of steel producers, to inform policy design (carbon price levels, free allowances, border adjustments) and corporate investment decisions in low-carbon steelmaking.

**Research date:** 2026-08-13 · **Sources analyzed:** 15 · **Evidence records:** 65 · **Verified claims:** 63

---

## Executive Summary

As of March 2025, China's national ETS expanded to steel at ~100 yuan/tCO2 (~$14/t), versus EU ETS prices of EUR 50–100/t (2023–2025) and US explicit carbon prices of just EUR 2.15/t (2023)—differentials implying large but unquantified cross-regional cost asymmetries. Strongest evidence: CBAM at EUR 65/tCO2 adds EUR 10–100/t crude steel in 2026, rising to EUR 100–200/t by 2034, with India's carbon-intensive exports most exposed. Biggest uncertainty: no verified per-tonne cost impacts by production route, pass-through effects, or technology breakeven prices. Recommendation: pair free, intensity-based allowance allocation with border adjustments to preserve competitiveness; corporates should build route-specific cost models before committing to low-carbon investments.
