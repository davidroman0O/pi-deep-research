# Assess the current size, leverage, and loss-concentration profile of the private credit market and stress its vulnerability to a 2008-style rapid rate shock, to inform allocation sizing and risk-management decisions.

**Research date:** 2026-09-15 · **Sources analyzed:** 13 · **Evidence records:** 72 · **Verified claims:** 71

---

## Executive Summary

US private credit (~$1.4T, end-2025) now rivals the leveraged loan market and supplies ~45% of lending to private nonfinancial corporations. The clearest stress signal is borrower-level: median EBITDA leverage of 6.1x, interest coverage of roughly 1.7x–2.0x, and >40% of borrowers with negative free cash flow — a thin cushion against a 2008-style rate shock. The critical uncertainty is fund-level leverage: NAV facilities (~$100B, projected toward ~$600B) and CFO structures are shifting toward concentrated portfolios, but no debt-to-equity or shock-tested loss data exist. Recommendation: size allocations conservatively and require rate-shock and fund-leverage disclosures before further deployment.

## Market size, growth, and competitive position

## Market size, growth, and competitive position

The US private credit market stood at approximately $1.4 trillion at end-2025 [9]. The US leveraged loan market was roughly equal in size, also about $1.4 trillion [9]. Together, the two markets supplied approximately 45% of total lending to private nonfinancial corporations at end-2025, with the remainder comprising bank loans, mortgages, finance company loans, and foreign loans (excluding operating leases) [9].

| Metric (US, end-2025) | Private credit | Leveraged loans |
|---|---|---|
| Market size | ~$1.4 trillion | ~$1.4 trillion |
| LBO/M&A share of issuance | 32% | 38% |

Source: [9]

Both markets have grown considerably since the Global Financial Crisis, with private credit expanding particularly rapidly in recent years [9]. Borrower-switching reinforces this trajectory: at the peak of the 2023 monetary tightening cycle, roughly 50% of borrowers switching between markets moved from leveraged loans to private credit, equivalent to about 25% of total private credit issuance [9].

Confidence in these figures is high (0.92), but they rest on a single source memo; no independent corroboration was available, and no global ex-US AUM figure could be verified.

## Contradictions Detected

- **Fitch calculates median EBITDA leverage for its US Privately Monitored Ratings (PMR) portfolio at 6.1x in 2024.** vs **Fitch estimates median EBITDA leverage for its privately monitored rating (PMR) portfolio at 5.9x in 2024.** — Both claims assert the same atomic proposition: Fitch's median EBITDA leverage for its US PMR portfolio in 2024. Same subject (Fitch PMR portfolio), same metric (median EBITDA leverage), same unit (x), same scope (US), and same reference year (2024). The only difference is the reported value: 6.1x vs 5.9x. Since the values are logically incompatible for the same quantity, the claims contradict each other. The differing report vintages (3Q25 monitor vs 1Q25 report) do not change the fact that both purport to state the 2024 figure for the same portfolio.
- **Fitch forecasts median interest coverage for its US PMR portfolio at 1.9x for 2025F.** vs **Fitch forecasts PMR portfolio median interest coverage will rise to 2.0x in 2025F from 1.7x in 2024.** — Both claims assert the same atomic proposition: Fitch's forecast of the median interest coverage for its US PMR portfolio in 2025F. They share the same subject (Fitch PMR portfolio), same scope (US), same metric (median interest coverage), same forecast year (2025F), and compatible conditions (forecast following Fed rate cuts). However, they state incompatible values: 1.9x vs 2.0x. Since the same subject, scope, date, and conditions yield logically opposed content, this is a true contradiction rather than a duplicate or a distinct reinforcing proposition.
- **Fitch forecasts diversified manufacturing median leverage in its US PMR portfolio to improve from 8.1x in 2024 to 6.0x in 2025F.** vs **Fitch forecasts diversified-manufacturing median leverage will drop to 6.1x in 2025F from 8.1x in 2024, driving PMR portfolio deleveraging.** — Both claims concern the same subject (Fitch PMR diversified-manufacturing median leverage), the same conditions (US PMR portfolio, 2025F vs 2024), and agree on the 2024 baseline of 8.1x. However, they assert different 2025F forecast values: 6.0x (Claim A) versus 6.1x (Claim B). Since the same metric under the same conditions cannot be both 6.0x and 6.1x, the claims are logically opposed and cannot both be true — a true contradiction.
- **Fitch forecasts consumer products median leverage in its US PMR portfolio to increase to 7.2x in 2025F, up from 6.1x in 2024.** vs **Fitch forecasts consumer-sector median leverage will rise to 7.1x in 2025F from 6.1x in 2024, the largest PMR portfolio increase, linked to weak consumer spending and rising defaults.** — Both claims describe the same subject (Fitch US PMR portfolio, consumer sector/products median leverage), the same 2024 baseline (6.1x), and the same forecast horizon (2025F). However, they assert different forecast values: Claim A says 7.2x while Claim B says 7.1x. Same subject, compatible conditions, and logically opposed content (different numerical values for the same metric) constitute a contradiction.
- **Fitch estimates median EBITDA leverage for its privately monitored rating (PMR) portfolio at 5.9x in 2024.** vs **Fitch forecasts PMR portfolio median leverage (debt/EBITDA) will decline to 5.7x in 2025F from 6.1x in 2024.** — Both claims concern the same subject (Fitch PMR portfolio, US middle market) and the same metric (median debt/EBITDA leverage) for the same year (2024), with compatible conditions. However, they assert different values for the 2024 median: Claim A states 5.9x, while Claim B states 6.1x as the 2024 actual baseline (before declining to 5.7x in 2025F). Both cannot be true for the same portfolio, scope, and year, so the claims are logically opposed on the shared 2024 figure.
- **Fitch estimates median EBITDA leverage for its privately monitored rating (PMR) portfolio at 5.9x in 2024.** vs **Fitch PMR portfolio median leverage (debt/EBITDA) was 6.1x in 2024.** — Both claims assert the same atomic proposition: the median debt/EBITDA leverage of the Fitch PMR portfolio (US middle market) for 2024. They share the same subject, metric (median debt/EBITDA), unit (x), scope, and date, but report incompatible values (5.9x vs 6.1x). Since 5.9 ≠ 6.1, the claims are logically opposed and cannot both be true, so they contradict.

**Base estimate:** 2.0x median EBITDA/interest coverage (Fitch US PMR 2025F forecast, Feb-2026; KBRA DLD quarterly average ~2.0x), vs 1.7x 2024 actual; PCDR already a record 6.3% TTM Aug-2026

| Scenario | Assumption | 2026 | 2027 | 2028 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative (no shock; easing path realized) | No 2008-style repricing: Fed easing path already embedded in Fitch forecasts holds (25bp cuts Sep/Oct 2025 already lifted coverage 1.7x->1.9x, i.e. ~0.2x per 50bp); rate relief continues ~0.1x/yr; PCDR drifts down from its 6.3% record as consumer-sector deleveraging (7.1x forecast leverage) stabilizes. | 2.0x | 2.1x | 2.2x |
